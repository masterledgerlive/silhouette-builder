The Gauntlet verdict

  • Work under the assumption, in an ASSUMED layer — adopted 7–0.
  • "P = NP means all computational crypto breaks" — TRUE-IN-PRINCIPLE, PRACTICE-CONDITIONAL, 7–0. A polynomial algorithm with astronomical constants changes theory, not practice. Levin's universal search is the standing proof of that gap.
  • One claim rejected 7–0: the brief called smart-contract verification "the halting problem, NP-complete." The halting problem is undecidable — a different beast. Bounded verification was adopted in its place. The Skeptic caught it; the room upheld him.
  • Anchors: dual-signing at genesis — ML-DSA (the finalized lattice standard) plus SLH-DSA (the finalized hash-based standard). Falcon waits: its standard was still draft. ML-DSA-44 signatures measure about 2,420 bytes — roughly 38 times an elliptic-curve signature; that size problem is real and counted.
  • Physical anchors adopted unconditionally — one-time keys, hardware roots of trust, threshold consensus: worth building in every world, assumed or not.
  • The roadmap dates in the brief (Ethereum post-quantum by about 2029, Base and Hedera about 2027) are graded CLAIMED — vendor roadmaps, not facts.

The Skeptic's concurrence, recorded in full: every adopted piece is worth building even if P is not NP. "Build the ark; don't preach the flood as fact."

The design — a Block with two layers

The PROVEN core certifies: hash chains, blob storage, chain anchors, post-quantum signatures. The ASSUMED Millennium Registry computes: P = NP, the Riemann Hypothesis, Navier–Stokes, Yang–Mills, Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer, and the Hodge conjecture — each carried as ASSUMED, each use stamped with what it was derived under, and no value ever gated on an assumed entry alone. Poincaré sits in the registry as the example of what graduation looks like: assumed for a century, proven, moved to the core. As classical and quantum computation improve, the ladder climbs: exact classical solving, heuristic and AI search, quantum acceleration only where it measurably wins, and a budgeted oracle simulator for rehearsing the assumed world.

First buildable slice: Registry version zero, the oracle-budget simulator, and the dual-anchor manifest format — local and testnet only.

The solve exercise — measured, not theorized

A toy hash was compiled into a satisfiability problem. The encoding grows linearly — thirteen variables and forty-five clauses per input bit, measured — and preimages were recovered and verified up to twenty bits. Then the wall, also measured: on hard random instances, median search grew from 28 nodes to over 19,000 as problems grew from forty to a hundred variables, and median time from three-thousandths of a second to twenty-one seconds. Real hash scale is far beyond tonight's solver. Under the standing instruction, the assumption stands regardless: the exercise locates our current hands, not the truth of the assumption.
