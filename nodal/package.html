THE PACKAGE

Dream Court investor research — location scouting, build costs & operating anchors. Clearwater / Largo / Pinellas County, FL.

DESIGN-STAGE RESEARCH — NOT AN OFFER, NOT A COMMITMENT. Prepared 2026-11-12 from public listing pages and published cost data only. No seller, broker, or agent was contacted. No site was visited. Every figure is sourced; ranges are labeled ESTIMATE. Where a figure could not be verified from a public source, it is marked UNVERIFIED rather than filled in.

0 · THE SHAPE OF THE PROBLEM

Indoor tennis has a hard physical filter that most industrial real estate fails:

Footprint per court: 120 ft × 60 ft — the ITF standard envelope for one court including run-back and side run-off. Two courts side by side ≈ 14,400 sq ft of clear playing hall before lobby, viewing, locker, and office space. (ITF court-size requirements; LTA dimensions summary — accessed 2026-11-12.)

Ceiling height: ITF minimum clear height at the net is 9.0 m (29.6 ft); indoor/show courts are guided to a 10 m (32.8 ft) top height. Height is measured to the lowest obstruction — light rigs, sprinklers, purlins — not the roof sheet.

What the local market actually offers: the overwhelming majority of existing Pinellas County warehouse stock clears 14–28 ft. Of 20+ listings reviewed, exactly one cluster — the new-build logistics parks in Pinellas Park (Gateway / Mid-Pinellas) — states a 32 ft clear height, which passes the ITF minimum with ~2.4 ft of margin.

The fastest real route to a compliant court is a lease in a new Pinellas Park logistics building, not a purchase. No for-sale listing found in Clearwater/Largo/St. Petersburg meets the height requirement at any price.

2 · BUILD COSTS

Design basis for all totals: 2 courts, leased shell of ~25,000–30,000 SF, conversion build-out (not ground-up), sensor layer built in-house (turnkey alternative priced separately). All totals are ESTIMATES assembled from the sourced line items below.

2a · Facility — Year 1

Scenario A (modeled headline): lease 25,000 SF in a new Pinellas Park logistics building (#1-type). Base rent anchor $12.35/SF/yr NNN (Q3 2026), new construction up to ~$14.50/SF; NNN pass-through modeled at $3.05–$4.50/SF (ESTIMATE; $3.05 was the published 2023 estimate on the Largo flyer in §1 #4). Year-1 occupancy, 25,000 SF: base rent $309k–$363k + NNN $76k–$113k ≈ $385k (low) / $430k (likely) / $475k (high).

Scenario B: lease the 55,350 SF Mid-Pinellas block (#2). ≈ $760k / $900k / $1.05M per year (ESTIMATE). Buys a 3–4 court growth path and event-scale space.

Scenario C: purchase. No compliant for-sale asset exists in the scouted set. A ground-up 2-court steel hall is a $1.5M–$3M-class structure decision before land (published 3–4 court permanent steel comparators; single-court 60×120 ft turnkey $161,200–$189,200, plus 20–40% beyond the building for flooring, lighting, HVAC and site work) — land cost in Pinellas not priced here (UNVERIFIED) and excluded from the headline totals, which use Scenario A.

Sources: Cushman & Wakefield MarketBeat Q1–Q3 2026; MetalBuildings.org; Avvale indoor tennis facility guide — accessed 2026-11-12.

2c · The sensor / compute layer — the part that makes it this facility

Buying officiating instead of building it: Hawk-Eye (Sony) equipment for one court is reported at ~$100,000 (10–12 cameras at 340 fps); Hawk-Eye Live implementation reported at ~$50,000 per court including software and technicians; ~$60,000 per court is the commonly cited figure — reported estimates; Hawk-Eye publishes no price list. Ongoing licensing/operations reported at $60,000–$100,000 per year per venue. Club-tier comparison — PlaySight SmartCourt: ~$10,000–$12,500 per court installed plus ~$500/month — the price anchor for "good club analytics," roughly 5–10× below Hawk-Eye class, and it does not claim tournament-grade line-calling. 3 · OPERATING SHAPE — real local rate anchors (anchors, NOT projections)

Innisbrook Resort, Palm Harbor (Pinellas County): court fee $30 per 2 hours ($15/hr), outdoor Har-Tru; private lessons from $40.

USTA National Campus, Orlando (public, subsidized — a floor, not a market rate): hard courts $20/hr, Har-Tru $24/hr.

Crowdsourced local check: Tampa court rental (1 hr, weekend) $16.20 (Numbeo, updated Nov 12, 2026 — directional only).

Indoor comparators: USTA Billie Jean King NTC indoor hard $42/hr off-peak to $82/hr prime (New York market — an indoor-premium ceiling, not a Tampa comp); Brown University indoor $29–$34/hr.

The analytics layer already has a market price: PlaySight-equipped facilities charge members ~$25/month for access to their tracking/video data, and PlaySight court time has been surcharged at $15/court-hour at club level — published evidence that players already pay a per-hour premium for exactly the form/video data the Dream Court sells as its core product.

Pinellas outdoor/resort court time clears $15–$24/hr. Indoor courts nationally clear $29–$82/hr. A defensible planning anchor for a premium, analytics-included indoor court in Tampa Bay is therefore $30–$60/hr court time, plus a data/analytics tier — a positioning judgment built on the anchors above, not a projection. Utilization is the variable no public source can give us; it must come from a local demand study before any investor number is quoted as expected revenue.

Cost-side anchor: year-round climate control for a small indoor court building is reported around ~$4,000/month in utilities; a 32-ft-clear leased hall in Florida humidity will sit at the demanding end — operating models should carry utilities as a named line, sized from the landlord's building data at diligence (currently UNVERIFIED). Sources as cited in the full package — accessed 2026-11-12.
