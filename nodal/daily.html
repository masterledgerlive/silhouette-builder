The whole day at a glance: every match on the wire gets our 1-2-3 call, its flags, and its watch-out ticker — the same vocabulary as the Cheat Sheets. Every flag traces to a computed number or a named tell; the rules are printed under the board. No vibes flags. One day at a time for now — the week view is the later extension.

The flag vocabulary — the exact rules

SHOE-IN our number for the called side is ≥ 93% — near-100, and only where the math earns it. The number always prints with it.
BLOWOUT our number ≥ 80% and the futures put a straight-sets finish at ≥ 55%.
HIGH RISK / GET OUT any of: the live reprice has run ≥ 25 points against the frozen pre-match favorite · the Grave is open (Set-1 loser down 0-2 in Set 2 — a counted 7.7% state) · a player carries WALK-OUT WATCH (≥ 3 retirements/walkovers-given on their career file).
PROMISE the ranking underdog whose sim number is ≥ 45% — the sim disagrees with the ranking story; ranks and number print.
UNDERVALUED a thin-data player (card under 25 matches, or tape-nodes only) whose sim number is ≥ 40% — always with the THIN tag showing. Thin data is never hidden.
The ticker strip beside each match carries the live tells as they exist: FLIP DIAL · GRAVE · EARLY-LEAD PRINT · DEAD-STOPPER · REVEAL WATCH (the counted Set-2 drop rate after that Set-1 score) · DECIDER NODE.

Our own records — sealed before outcomes

The desk runs this same board on its side and seals every upcoming and live call — sim number, pick, flags, ticker — into the hash-chained ledger before the outcomes exist, the same chain the slate matches finalize into. After matches finish, the grades land as their own records (pick right or wrong, Brier), and the board's totals are read from the ledger alone. Nobody can edit a call after the fact: the chain re-verifies end to end, and a changed record breaks it loudly.
