## 0. THE SHAPE OF THE PROBLEM (read this first)

Indoor tennis has a hard physical filter that most industrial real estate fails:

- **Footprint per court:** 120 ft × 60 ft (36.6 m × 18.3 m) — the ITF/LTA standard envelope for one court including run-back (6.40 m behind baselines) and side run-off (3.66 m). Two courts side by side ≈ 120 ft × 120 ft ≈ 14,400 sq ft of clear playing hall before lobby, viewing, locker, and office space. Source: ITF court-size requirements, https://www.itftennis.com/media/9026/court-size-org-requirements.pdf (accessed 2026-11-12); LTA dimensions summary, https://join.playmore.co/blog/tennis-court-dimensions/ (accessed 2026-11-12).
- **Ceiling height:** ITF minimum clear height at the net is **9.0 m (29.6 ft)**; 5.75 m at the baseline; 4 m at the back of the run-back. Indoor/show courts are guided to a **10 m (32.8 ft) top height**. Height is measured to the *lowest obstruction* (light rigs, sprinklers, purlins), not the roof sheet. Sources: ITF, https://www.itftennis.com/media/9026/court-size-org-requirements.pdf; LTA via Playmore, https://join.playmore.co/blog/tennis-court-dimensions/ (both accessed 2026-11-12).
- **What the local market actually offers:** the overwhelming majority of existing Pinellas County warehouse stock clears **14–28 ft**. Of 20+ listings reviewed, exactly **one cluster of properties — the new-build logistics parks in Pinellas Park (Gateway / Mid-Pinellas) — states a 32 ft clear height**, which passes the ITF 9.0 m minimum with ~2.4 ft of margin (and sits ~0.8 ft under the 10 m show-court ideal; lighting must be high-bay/flush mounted to preserve clearance — diligence item, not a deal-breaker).

**Bottom line up front:** the fastest real route to a compliant court is a **lease in a new Pinellas Park logistics building**, not a purchase. No for-sale listing found in Clearwater/Largo/St. Petersburg meets the height requirement at any price (details and near-misses in §1).

## 2. BUILD COSTS

Design basis for all totals: **2 courts**, leased shell of ~25,000–30,000 SF, conversion build-out (not ground-up construction), sensor layer built in-house (with the turnkey alternative priced separately). All totals are ESTIMATES assembled from the sourced line items below.

### 2a. Facility — Year 1

**Scenario A (modeled headline): lease 25,000 SF in a new Pinellas Park logistics building (#1-type).**
- Base rent anchor: Gateway–Mid Pinellas W/D asking **$12.35/SF/yr NNN** (Q3 2026); new-construction space commands up to **~$14.50/SF** (Cushman & Wakefield Q1 2026 note). Pinellas County overall asking $11.86–$11.98/SF (C&W Q2/Q3 2026). Sources as cited in §1.
- NNN/operating pass-through anchor: $3.05/SF was the published 2023 estimate on the Largo flyer in §1 #4; modeled at $3.05–$4.50/SF (ESTIMATE).
- **Year-1 occupancy, 25,000 SF: base rent $309k–$363k + NNN $76k–$113k ≈ $385k (low) / $430k (likely) / $475k (high).**

**Scenario B: lease the 55,350 SF Mid-Pinellas block (#2).** At $11.00–$13.69/SF base (Pinellas range incl. Gateway W/D $13.69 top, C&W Q3 2026) + NNN: **≈ $760k (low) / $900k (likely) / $1.05M (high) per year** (ESTIMATE). Buys a 3–4 court growth path and event-scale space.

**Scenario C: purchase.** No compliant for-sale asset exists in the scouted set (see §1). The nearest priced asset is $5,995,000 (#5) and it fails height; a ground-up steel hall is the real purchase comparator: a single-court 60×120 ft building runs ~$105,900–$124,400 kit-only or **$161,200–$189,200 turnkey** (foundation + erection), with buildings over 60 ft wide carrying a clear-span premium that per-SF pricing understates, plus 20–40% beyond the building for flooring, lighting, HVAC and site work (MetalBuildings.org, https://www.metalbuildings.org/commercial/sports-facilities/ , accessed 2026-11-12). A 2-court ground-up project is therefore a **$1.5M–$3M-class structure decision before land** (consistent with published 3–4 court permanent steel at $1.5M–$3M, Avvale indoor tennis facility guide, https://avvale.co.uk/pages/indoor-tennis-facility-business-plan-template , accessed 2026-11-12) — **land cost in Pinellas not priced here (UNVERIFIED)** and excluded from the headline totals, which use Scenario A.

### 2c. The sensor / compute layer (the part that makes it *this* facility)

**The comparison point — buying officiating instead of building it:**
- **Hawk-Eye (Sony) tennis:** equipment for one court reported at **~$100,000** with ~3-day install/calibration, 10–12 cameras at 340 fps, 3.6 mm stated accuracy (CNBC reporting via UIS Journal, https://uisjournal.com/how-does-the-hawk-eye-work-in-tennis-and-how-much-is-the-hawk-eye-more-precise-than-a-line-judge/ , accessed 2026-11-12). Hawk-Eye Live implementation reported at **~$50,000 per court** including software and technicians (ESPN via UbiTennis, https://www.ubitennis.net/2020/10/time-to-axe-line-judges-from-matches-says-novak-djokovic/ , accessed 2026-11-12); ~$60,000 per court is the commonly cited figure (multiple secondary sources, e.g. https://research.leaguesportsco.com/reports/end-of-self-reported-scores-ai-verification-trust-layer/ , accessed 2026-11-12). Ongoing licensing/operations reported at $60,000–$100,000 per year per venue and one-week tournament rental at $20,000–$30,000 (secondary cost guide, https://eathealthy365.com/how-much-does-a-hawk-eye-system-cost/ , accessed 2026-11-12 — **treat as reported estimates; Hawk-Eye publishes no price list**).
- **Club-tier comparison — PlaySight SmartCourt:** ~**$10,000–$12,500 per court** installed plus ~$500/month (Kiosk Marketplace interview with PlaySight VP Product, https://www.kioskmarketplace.com/articles/game-set-match-the-kiosk-as-tennis-coach/ ; League Sports research, URL above; a 6-court indoor SmartCourt install was quoted ~$110,000 in a 2016 college-tennis report, https://www.collegetennistoday.com/2016/01/04/sec-to-stream-again/ — all accessed 2026-11-12). This is the price anchor for "good club analytics," roughly 5–10× below Hawk-Eye class — and it does not claim tournament-grade line-calling.

**Building our own mesh (modeled configuration, stated assumptions):**
- **Camera grid: 12 high-speed global-shutter cameras per court** (matching Hawk-Eye's 10–12 camera geometry). Component pricing spans two worlds: commodity 1000 fps machine-vision cameras list at **$320–$800** (Accio marketplace listings, https://www.accio.com/plp/high-speed-industrial-usb-computer-camera-for-laptop , accessed 2026-11-12); scientific-grade 1000 fps cameras run **$13,500–$27,000 each** (same marketplace data). The model uses tracking-grade mid units at **$1,500–$5,000 each (ESTIMATE)** → **$18,000–$60,000 per court in cameras**.
- **LiDAR: 6 units facility-wide** (corners + above-net positions for player-form capture). Pricing anchors: Ouster OS-class units listed **$6,683–$28,400** on marketplace (Accio, https://www.accio.com/plp/at128p , accessed 2026-11-12); industrial/robotics LiDAR ASPs run **~$500–$3,000** per sensor (Longbridge Ouster analysis, https://longbridge.com/quote/KMTUY.US/topics/100000000687954?channel=n280767921 , accessed 2026-11-12); automotive-grade units now under $500 (Robotics China Insider, https://www.robotichina.com/hesai-vs-velodyne.html , accessed 2026-11-12). Modeled at $500–$7,000/unit → **$3,000–$42,000**.
- **Compute:** 2–4 GPU edge servers + storage for multi-camera 340 fps ingest — **$30,000–$120,000 (ESTIMATE — no public vendor quote retrieved for this configuration; marked UNVERIFIED as a priced line, included because the layer does not function without it).**
- **Networking, sync, mounting, cabling:** $15,000–$40,000 (ESTIMATE).
- **Calibration, integration, verdict/analytics software build allocation:** $50,000–$150,000 (ESTIMATE — internal build cost allowance, not a vendor quote).
- **Back-wall LED screen:** assumed **40 ft × 12 ft = 480 SF** spanning the back of the show court. Installed dvLED pricing by pitch (2026, panels + processing + structure + install): **P3–P4 indoor $500–$900/SF; P2.5 $900–$1,600/SF** (CrownTV 2026 price guide, https://www.crowntv-us.com/blog/led-video-wall-cost/ ; corroborated by ITS, https://www.itouchinc.com/video-wall-cost-breakdown-hardware-installation-content-and-ongoing-management , both accessed 2026-11-12). Hardware-only event-grade kits run ~$220–$300/SF (same CrownTV guide). **Modeled: $106,000–$144,000 (kit/low) / ~$300,000 (likely, P3–P4 installed mid-range) / $432,000 (high, P3–P4 installed top).** A P2.5 wall would add ~$200k+ over the high case — not modeled in the headline.

**Sensor/compute layer total, 2 courts (c): ≈ $250,000 (low) / $585,000 (likely) / $900,000 (high).**
(Turnkey alternative for comparison: Hawk-Eye-class install ≈ $100,000–$200,000 for 2 courts + a reported $60,000–$100,000/yr license/operations — cheaper in Year 1, but it is a rented verdict service: no owned mesh, no form-analytics product, no back-wall content engine. The build-our-own layer is the asset; that is the honest trade the package is asking investors to price.)
