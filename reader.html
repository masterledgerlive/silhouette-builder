The key is not in the photograph. It is under the scratch panel on the card itself, which is the whole point — see below.
READ THIS BEFORE TRUSTING A PHOTO.

A photograph cannot prove an object. Anything printed can be photocopied, and the copy scans identically — same QR, same serial, same everything. So "one picture documents the card's legitimacy" is not true on its own, and it is worth knowing that now rather than after a dispute.

What does work, and it is the design you were already reaching for:
1 · The serial is public — printed, scannable, identifies which card this is.
2 · The claim key is secret — printed under the scratch panel, so it is not in any photograph of an unopened card.
3 · The ledger is authoritative — the first person to redeem that key owns it. A photocopy scans the same and claims nothing, because the key was already spent.

That is why the scratch panel matters. It is not decoration — it is the half of the system that a camera cannot capture. The picture proves which card; the sealed key proves this card.

You said the constant "can be mimicked" — that instinct was right, and it is exactly why the key has to be rotatable on transfer. On sale the seller reveals the key, the buyer redeems it, and the ledger issues a new one. The old key is dead the moment it is used.
What this can and cannot read.

The standard symbologies — QR, UPC, EAN, Code 128, Data Matrix, PDF417, Aztec — are decoded by the browser itself, with no library downloaded at all. That is the honest answer to "is there already a system": yes, and it is already on the phone.

The DAVID colour grid, Morse marks, punch holes and Braille are NOT readable here yet. They are our own encodings, so no browser and no app knows them. Reading one from a photo needs a step nothing has built yet: find the card in the picture, work out its corners, undo the perspective, and only then sample the cells. That is the next real piece of work, and it is a genuine piece of work — not a switch to flip.

Google Lens did not miss your hidden data. It OCR'd the card text and image-searched the artwork, because that is all Lens does. To Lens a colour grid is texture.
