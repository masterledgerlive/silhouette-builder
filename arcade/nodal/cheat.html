CHEAT SHEETS
Every mathematical cheat sheet the desk owns, in one room — probability by set, by state, by point. Nothing on this page is invented: each sheet names its source file and its sample. Three kinds of truth live here and are labeled as such — CENSUS (counted from the tour archive), SLAM TAPE (counted from point-by-point Slam data), and HOUSE RULE (the desk's entry/exit law — a rule, not a statistic). Where the desk tested a tell and it failed, the tell is shown dead. Where the desk holds no data, the gap is printed, not papered over.

Sheet 3 — Live Set 2 states · the grave, the print, the serve-out

Source: PATTERNS.md (P-008) and nodal/tells.py — states inside the second set, counted on the same archive.

☠ THE GRAVE — 7.7%

S1 loser who also trails 0-2 in Set 2: wins the set 11.0% · wins the match 7.7% (n=2,038). P-008 graded the hopeful reading of this state and killed it: it is a grave, not a trap. On the desk this is a salvage question, not a hold. The states around it: at 1-2 down — set 20.4% / match 13.4%; at 2-2 — set 37.1% / match 22.4%, still below the 2-1 control (47.2%).

⚑ THE EARLY-LEAD PRINT — ~70%

S1 loser who leads Set 2 by 2-0: takes Set 2 69.3–72.1% of the time (PATTERNS.md P-008; tells.py rounds it to ~70%). This is the strongest "the flip is live" read the desk owns — the exact mirror image of the Grave, one break of serve apart. Direction, not hope: when the print fires, the turnaround is the statistical side.

Sheet 4 — Turning points · entry / exit

HOUSE RULES — the desk's law, not census statistics. These are the rules the overlay trades by; they were written off live matches and are labeled as rules wherever they appear in the desk.

CLEAR SIGN — ENTRY. Act only on an obvious zone: the model's forward price at ≥88%, or a market price ≥15¢ under the model's fair with the modal path still intact. Anything else is NO CLEAR SIGN — no position, no apology. The 2026-11-06 worked example on Sheet 6 sat ~14¢ under fair and was correctly left alone: close is not a sign.

SERVE-OUT CASH — EXIT. Priced 88–95 with a serve-out game pending and a documented re-open cell behind it → the peak IS the exit. Written 2026-11-04 off a live flip: a leader priced 91 serving for the match was called "roughly fair" at the desk, got broken back, and the rule was carved from the miss. When the serve-out and the price band arrive together, cash — do not wait for the last points.

THE 25-POINT FALL — EXIT. When the live reprice falls ≥25 points against the frozen favorite, the position's story is over — exit fast. This is the same ≥25-point move the watch loop uses as its reporting trigger: what is loud enough to report is loud enough to leave.

SATO RULE — PRIORITY. A live position's exit watch outranks all other desk work. Turning-point sheets are read for the position you hold before the match you fancy.

Sheet 6 — Finite moves · the checkmate routes

Source: nodal/price_state.py — the desk's exact dynamic-programming repricer. A tennis match is a finite game: from any live state there is a countable set of routes to the end, and the DP walks all of them.

How it deals: two point probabilities — each player's chance of winning a point on their own serve, read off their cards (serve vs the opponent's return: A serving vs B = (A.serve + (100 − B.return)) / 2). From those two numbers the recursion builds, exactly: point → game (win 4-0 / 4-1 / 4-2, then the deuce race p²/(p²+q²)) → tiebreak (serve pattern 1, then 2s) → set (games recursion, tiebreak at 6-6) → match (first to 2 sets). No simulation, no sampling noise: every remaining route is enumerated and weighted, so the price for this exact state — sets, games, next server — is exact for the two inputs. Change the state by one game and the whole board re-deals in milliseconds.

Stop-in-tracks cells — where the DP and the census agree a route has collapsed

☠ THE GRAVE: down a set, 0-2 in S2 — match 7.7% (P-008)Dead-stopper at 0: S1 winner held to 0 in lost S2 — decider 39.7%Dead-stopper at 2: decider 44.4% — the dealer of the 2 owns the third, 55.6%Cross 5,1 cell: S1 winner's decider % 33.3 — the coldest readable cell in THE CROSS

These are the checkmate squares: not "likely to lose" — cells where every enumerated route forward is a narrow door, and both instruments (the counted census and the exact DP) point the same way.

Pace without a clock

The desk holds no timing feed, so pace is read from the point record itself. Clean, fast rounds: holds to love or 15, aces clustering, returner winning ~0 points — the server's games shrink and the route steepens toward the serve-out cells. Grinding rounds: every service game reaching deuce or break point, second serves multiplying, DFs arriving in pairs. Neither is a verdict alone — pace is the speed at which a match travels between the cells on Sheets 1–3, and it is read per player, off their card, against their own base rates. Any numeric pace or fault threshold the desk applies live is a HOUSE RULE reading, labeled as such at the time — never a census fact.

Sheet 8 — Who's actually stronger · the setup detector

David's question: who is setting whom up — who "lost" a set they were controlling? Honesty first: the math shows a shape, never an intention. No sheet here can prove a set was thrown on purpose, and this room does not claim it can. What the sheets can do is flag "stronger than the scoreboard" — a trailer whose underlying numbers are winning while the sets say otherwise. Three flags, all sourced:

FlagThe mathSource
(a) The quiet winner of the pointsThe S1 loser whose point-level record is not losing: serve % not decaying across the match thirds, pressure deltas (Sheet 7) at or above their own card base. The sets went one way; the points didn't.point_profiles.json fields + Sheet 5 decay frame
(b) The cruise-then-caught cellThe leader won S1 by 6-1 / 6-2 — the two most catchable scores on the census (lose S2 28.0% / 29.5%) — and the caught set, when it comes, is a cheap dump (≤2 games) 21.9–23.6% of the time. A cheap second set after a cruise is a shape with a name, not a mystery.COMBO-KEY.md P-010
(c) The crossing thirdsThe trailer's point-win % is rising by match thirds while the leader's serve-points-won decays ≥4–5 pts from his own early level. At Slam level the eventual winner rises +4.2 and the loser falls −4.2 (serve: −4.8) — when the crossing runs the other way, the scoreboard is behind the match.COMBO-KEY.md P-010, Slam tape n=647

Then adjust by the player's own shape signature (PROFILES.md — player cards, same archive)

Straight-vs-setup is a player signature, not a match state (COMBO-KEY.md P-010). Field averages: V-recovery (wins that are LWW) 18.5% · sawtooth (wins that are WLW) 16.3% · S2 let-go (won S1, lost S2) ~30% · closers (wins in straights) 65.3%. Against those fields: Borges V-recovery 37.3% of wins (PROFILES.md / COMBO-KEY.md) · J.M. Cerundolo sawtooth 48.1% (COMBO-KEY.md) · Dart S2 let-go 60.0% vs Swiatek 13.9% (PROFILES.md). A sawtooth player's dropped second set is their normal route; a closer's dropped second set is an event. The sheet anchor is the census number; the signature says when the anchor is wrong for this player — and a live pattern outranks the file (Case B law, COMBO-KEY.md).

The detector's verdict form: flags (a)+(b), or (a)+(c), on the same match = stronger than the scoreboard — the trailer is priced as a loser and playing as the winner, and the routes on Sheet 6 should be re-dealt with the trailer's point inputs, not the set score's story. One flag alone = a watch, not a call. And the word stays shape: players cruise, tire, tank a return game to save legs, or simply get caught — the sheet cannot separate those, and says so.
