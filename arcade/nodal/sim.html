What the loop found — the headline: the lanes are not equal everywhere, and the board can prove it. On the week-to-week tour the pure card rules (LEDGER 62.6% picks) and the defense lane loses money it never had (FLIP 46.1% — its dog tweak is too hot against ordinary fields). At the Slams it inverts: late-round cards name the wrong favorite more often than not (LEDGER 45.3%), and FLIP — priced for exactly that failure — takes 65.1% with the best truth score, and wins the game-by-game race too. CHARGE pays for its confidence in both worlds: same picks as LEDGER, worse Brier everywhere. Temperament × arena is a real, measurable thing — which is the entire reason three lanes keep three separate books.

The voices — a plugin, never a gate

The text is the show. Every call is written first — it is the caption track, the sealed record, and what the board grades. Voices render the same lines; they never change a number.
LEDGER — voice NODAL Ledger (saved, slot 3 of the house's 10): the calm statistician.
CHARGE — voice NODAL Charge (saved): the play-by-play that rides the favorite.
FLIP — voice NODAL Flip (saved): the deadpan warning.
How a viewer hears them: the Voice Pass (our cast voices, rendered live per call — game-level calls fit voice latency today), or bring-your-own-key (a viewer's own ElevenLabs key renders the same text on their account). The math stays free either way; the voices are the upgrade. Config: nodal/voice-plugin.json · renderer: nodal/speak.py.
Laws held in both trials: walk-forward only · thin cards skipped (131 + 178 in A; 23 + 44 in B) · retirements excluded from Sim A · lanes differ only by the printed 22% tweak · every sim prediction sealed into the same hash chain as the live book, labeled SIM. Sim B's race prices each game from pre-match cards only. The full engine (rank bands + route engine) is what the live registry runs; these trials grade the lanes over walk-forward quick cards — the honest label, on purpose.
