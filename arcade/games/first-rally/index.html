1 · History & credit to the creators

In 1972, play got a ball. Ralph H. Baer, the inventor of home video games, put table tennis on a television with the Magnavox Odyssey — two paddles, one square ball, and a whole industry in embryo. That same year, engineer Allan Alcorn built Pong at a young company called Atari, founded by Nolan Bushnell and Ted Dabney. Pong's first test cabinet, in Andy Capp's Tavern, broke down because its coin box overflowed with quarters — the first arcade phenomenon was born.

History adds an honest wrinkle: Magnavox sued over Baer's patents, and Atari settled — the first proof in court that a game mechanic is a creation with an author. FIRST RALLY is an original Arcades of Time engine built from that mechanic — no ROM, no copied code — and the creators are named here because naming them is part of the game. Credit: Baer; Alcorn; Bushnell & Dabney.

2 · The mechanics

You hold the left paddle. First to 5 points wins (90-second cap). Move with ↑ / ↓ arrow keys (or W/S, or hold the touch pads). Your paddle speed is fixed — the only variable is you.

· Angle is a weapon: where the ball strikes your paddle bends its return — center hits fly flat, edge hits fly sharp.
· The mix-up: the seed deals your opponent every run — its tracking speed, its reaction lag, its freeze-error — and deals every serve. A memorized pattern plays against a different opponent each time and dies.
· Score = your points ×1000 − conceded ×100 + win bonus 500 + rally hits ×5. Your identity is your wallet — wallet is the who.

3 · The physics & the proof

This engine obeys the Physics Law: every parameter is an integer, the ball is integrated tick by tick on a fixed 16 ms step — no floating point, no device on Earth can replay it differently. What is recorded is your input tape (run-length encoded: direction × ticks). Your score is derived by replaying seed + tape against the dealt opponent, chained by SHA-256 — never trusted, always re-computed. If your browser hitches mid-match, the game slows but the tape stays exact: the replay runs at engine speed, not wall-clock speed.

Agentic AI plays under the same law. The Predictor parks at center and trusts its read of the serve — your paddle angles will walk straight past it. The Reactor tracks and reacts, and it can win. Beat both, and your score goes into the blob score chain — when fins are real, a part of every play funds keeping the top ten on chain for good. The players pay to be remembered; the chain keeps them.
