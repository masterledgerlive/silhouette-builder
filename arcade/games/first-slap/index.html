1 · History & credit to the creators

Before video games there were folk games of pure reaction. Slapjack — a traditional card game, author unknown, passed hand to hand for generations — runs on one law: see the jack, slap it first. In 1962 Steve Russell and colleagues at MIT built Spacewar!, the first widely played computer game, and reaction became digital. The arcade era that followed — the cabinets, the NES (Nintendo, 1983) with its D-pad and A/B buttons — turned reaction time into the world's first high-score culture.

FIRST SLAP is an original Arcades of Time study game. We claim no one else's game — we study the mechanic the folk players and those first programmers discovered: reaction, proven. Credit: the unnamed folk players of Slapjack; Russell and the Spacewar! team; Nintendo for the button vocabulary this cabinet speaks.

2 · The mechanics

Ten hands. Each hand, a target direction (◀ ▲ ▼ ▶) flashes at a secret, seed-dealt moment. Slap the button that means that direction after it flashes:

· Slap early = foul, hand lost. · Wrong button = miss. · Slap within 1,000 ms = you win the hand, scoring 1000 − your reaction ms.
· The mix-up: your buttons are remapped every run — the card on screen shows what each button means this run. Memorized combinations are worthless. Eyes and reaction are everything.
· Play with arrow keys or tap the pads. Your identity is your wallet — wallet is the who.

3 · The proof

This game runs on the Engine Law: fixed millisecond tick, integer math, seeded but true to the field. Your device plays at full speed — zero network lag. What is recorded is your input tape: every slap with its tick. Your score and every reaction time are derived by replaying that tape, chained by SHA-256 — never trusted, always re-computed. Your run key is sha256(wallet | seed | nonce): the handshake that says this play was proofed. The game files themselves live on Walrus blobs (see Blob Boot above) — anyone can spin this cabinet up from the blobs and replay your run as a rerun.
