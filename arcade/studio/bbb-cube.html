🧊🐰 BUNNY CUBE — the meadow, alive

A whole new Bunny game, and a different species from the 2D cabinet (which stays frozen as a first attempt, per the versioning law). This is a 3D open world: the film's own 2008 production models — Bunny, Frank, Rinky, Gamera, the apple tree, even the meadow terrain — converted once from the original .blend artifacts into game objects, blobbed to Walrus, sha256-verified in your browser, and assembled into the cube. You enter through THE DOOR — and this time the meadow fights back: BONK THE BULLIES, the film's own story. Frank, Rinky and Gamera pelt Bunny with apples; grab apples, bonk them back. First to the banner takes the round.

🌳 WHAT YOU'RE STANDING IN

Every object in this world is a Walrus blob with a printed record — name (blob id), key, proof (sha256), measure (bytes). The cast and the set were already rendered in 2008 for the film Big Buck Bunny (Blender Foundation, CC-BY 3.0): this desk's one conversion pass — Blender, headless, at the artifact→object boundary — baked the rigs at game weight (subsurf 1, widgets and cages excluded) into glTF objects. The game was then injected into the cube: one manifest names every object, and this page proves each one before it appears. The 12 apples hanging in the tree sit at the exact points the 2008 layout artists marked with empties in the tree file. New in v1.1: the ground under your feet is sampled from the terrain blob's own verified geometry — the meadow is a high plateau (the film's relief runs about 11 units), and the page now stands on the real numbers. New in v1.2: the camera finally looks through its own lens — v1.1 aimed the camera's frame at Bunny, which faced the lens the other way — and a guard raycast now patrols the lens, lifting it out of the hill if it ever slips again.
New in v1.3: the confession — the aim was never the whole story. A-Frame had seated its own default camera as the active one, rendering from inside the hill, while our camera sat aimed and unused. The page now claims the lens for its house camera at boot and prints the proof (the 🎥 line) in the log below.
New in v1.4: a fixed control pad (bottom-left) — David asked for a pad his thumb can find on the phone; it drives the same stick channel, with the gesture defended so iOS can't steal the hold as a scroll.
New in v1.5: a front door like the pictures he sent (The Shining / Stand By Me concept menus, with a Red Dead black-and-red heart): a start screen over the living meadow with a big PLAY button — the round waits for the press — plus a live map of the meadow (top-right) so you can find the players: bullies initialed, apples, the tree, THE DOOR, and a gold objective line that names the nearest bully, its distance, and which way to turn.
New in v1.6: the 2-PLAYER LINK TEST. One phone hosts the meadow (its truth is the truth); player 2 joins by room code as Bunny Two or as one of the bad guys. The relay carries only whispers — a wish direction, a throw — and the host answers with hash-chained sealed snapshots, which the guest's phone verifies one by one instead of trusting. The world itself never travels: both phones already hold it, from the blobs.
And it shows its work: tap the 🔗 badge for TIMING & PROOF — relay round-trip in milliseconds, snapshot interval and delay, guest-input→applied ms, seal-verify time on the guest's own phone, a timed control-point log (join, throw, pickup, each hit with its strike zone), and the relay's total byte count next to the world's provenance (manifest, blob count, megabytes, hashes verified at boot). GitHub serves only this page's envelope; the meadow arrives from Walrus. The host panel also deals a QR for the same-room test — the code carries the room and the world's address, never the world — and hosts announce an open table in the 🌍 lobby every 5 s, chess-challenge style, for the anywhere test.
