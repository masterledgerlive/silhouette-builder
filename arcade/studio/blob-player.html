2 · HOW A PLAY IS PROVEN — the highscore machine

A highscore here is never claimed — it is re-derived. Four steps, all visible on this page:

🎞️ 1 · THE TAPE
Every input is recorded as frame → direction (57:−1, 105:0, …). Keys are never trusted — they are data, replayed later.
🔁 2 · THE REPLAY
A cold engine — fetched fresh from its blob — runs the same seed + same tape. Ball, paddle, every hit and miss are recomputed, not read.
⛓️ 3 · THE HASH CHAIN
At frames 180/360/540/720/900 the sim hashes its whole state. If replay's chain equals the live chain, the play could not have been faked — any altered key changes the hash.
🏆 4 · THE RECORD
Score + tape + chain are written as a blob. One record format for every game and every machine — that's what keeps the highscores unified: anyone, anywhere, replays the blob and checks.
🏆 HIGHSCORE RECORD — run the proof (🧪 button above) and this card fills with the real record: score, chain, tape, and the latencies — how long the live run took, how long the cold replay took, and the blob recall time. Those three numbers are the whole verification cost.
Investor Q&A — the exact questions, answered plainly:
How do you verify a key press? You don't trust it — you replay it. The tape says "frame 233: left." The replay engine presses left at frame 233 and the paddle's movement falls out of the physics. A forged tape produces a different hash chain and is refused.
How do you verify a hit? A hit is not an event anyone reports; it's geometry the sim recomputes — ball position vs paddle position at that frame. If the replay doesn't produce the hit, the score doesn't exist.
What's the latency? Blob recall ≈ a tenth of a second (time it yourself with the RECALL TEST). A full 900-frame replay verifies in about the time the run itself took — and it can be checked by any machine, any time, forever, because the record is a blob.
Why is it unified? Every machine plays from the same content-addressed blobs (same address = same bytes = same game), and every score is the same kind of record: seed + tape + chain. One format, one check, every machine.

♻️ THE CONTINUATION EXHIBIT — a film that died, and came back as itself

Below, this page fetches both Big Buck Bunny manifests — the dissolved October original and today's revival — and compares them live. A blob's address is derived from its content, so identical bytes always mean the identical address; a re-blob is a renewal, not a copy. The Bunny's revival re-encrypted with fresh random nonces (new addresses), but its identity — the film hash, every section hash, every key — is checked against the original right here.

comparing manifests…

How a keeper re-blobs: the encrypted section files are kept locally (all 222 Robin Hood sections, and — lesson learned — all 78 Bunny sections as of today). Before expiry, the keeper simply re-PUTs the identical bytes; Walrus returns the identical blobId with a fresh lifetime. No re-encryption, no new addresses, no broken links. Robin Hood has been renewed this way since October. Storage here is Walrus testnet, rented in epochs (1 epoch ≈ 1 day on testnet); the same design on mainnet rents ~2-week epochs.

5 · THE CASCADE — RECALL THAT RECALLS ITSELF ♻️

The never-lost machinery, designed on air in The Cascade podcast and built to its contract. Two objects: the MOTHER REEL — one blob that names every manifest in the house — and the GROWING REEL — a hash-chained proof batch recording every birth, audit, renewal, death and revival, each link sealing the last. Its SNARK slot sits empty at genesis, labeled, until a real prover earns it. Recall climbs a ladder now: two independent mirrors before anything is declared lost — and a lost blob is healed by re-uploading its identical bytes, which restores the same address.
