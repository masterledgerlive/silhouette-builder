How it works, briefly

Two lanes — a verified data lane and a WireGuard tunnel lane. The chain is the notary and the directory, never the switch: it receipts sessions (~0.4 s finality) instead of pretending to forward packets. Sessions are planned in a blob whose hash names the session; out- and return-receipts pair up and Merkle-batch into a daily quilt; relevance is judged by verified reads from independent sources (a diversity floor, so one loud source can't keep junk alive); blobs are born dying and keepers extend what still matters before expiry — or it's gone, in public, with a tombstone. A deterministic policy engine decides; an AI may explain a plan but can never approve one.

The headline metric is the old-internet share of each route — and it only goes down on receipts.

Safety & law

Relay ≠ exit: in this phase relays coordinate and announce — nobody carries anyone else's traffic. When traffic phases arrive, public egress is designed for professional gateways only, never homes, with signed consent proofs; residential bandwidth resale is not part of this design. Operating real VPN infrastructure later means your jurisdiction's rules are yours to follow — the public launch is gated on legal review for exactly that reason. And never share a seed phrase or mainnet key with this kit, this page, or any AI prompt: testnet keys only.
